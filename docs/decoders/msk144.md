# MSK144

Meteor scatter in the mode **MSK144** of WSJT-X: short bursts (pings) reflected from the ionised trails of meteors,
from a tenth of a second to several seconds, on 6 m and 2 m (and 1.25 m, 70 cm). RADIMCOM has its own decoder
(`msk144.py`); it needs no time slots and no exact PC clock.

**DECODERS › MSK144** (DEC key):

- **ON** and the **band keys** 50.260, 50.280, 144.150, 144.360, 222.100 and 432.360 MHz (the dial frequencies used in
  Europe and North America, USB); a key tunes the active VFO there in USB and switches the decoder on. The signals are
  1400–1600 Hz above the dial.
- **Table**, newest on top: UTC, SNR of the ping (dB in 2.5 kHz), DF (audio frequency, Hz), **×** how many times the same
  message was decoded within 10 s (and how many 72 ms frames were averaged), the frequency and the message, the call
  highlighted. **LOG** writes a daily log (`data\logs\msk144\YYYY\MM\DD\msk144_YYYY-MM-DD.txt`), **CLR** clears the
  table.

## How it works

- The audio-band IQ of the VFO is mixed down by 1500 Hz and resampled to 12 kHz complex (as WSJT-X).
- Windows of 288 ms (4 frames) every 144 ms are tested for a ping: the squared signal of MSK has two spectral lines at
  ±1000 Hz (+ twice the offset) standing over the in-band median, or one 72 ms part is 1.4× louder than the noise.
- For a ping the frequency is found by the sync words (8 bits, twice a frame) on a 4 Hz grid, the frame is taken
  around the loudest part (cyclically, a frame repeats every 72 ms) and rolled onto its sync; frames of a long ping are
  added coherently. Both polarities are tried.
- 128 soft bits go to the LDPC (128,90) decoder (belief propagation, up to 10 iterations); the CRC-13 must agree, then
  the 77-bit message is unpacked (standard messages with calls, locators, reports, contest exchanges).
- Not decoded: short messages `<CALL1 CALL2>` (MSK40) and free text.
- Simulated: a 0.3 s ping decodes from about 5 dB SNR in 2.5 kHz, a 1 s ping from about 3 dB; 60 s of noise gave no
  false decode (`tests/test_msk144.py`). Not yet tried on the air.
