HFDL (High Frequency Data Link): ACARS of aircraft over oceans and remote areas, plus logons, positions and
        frequency data, decoded by dumphfdl. Frequencies: 2.9–22 MHz, 16 ground stations, each on a few channels
        (the table below); the band buttons centre the VFO on a band.
        The audio of the VFO is muted while this decoder runs: all channels within ±(sample rate / 2) of the VFO centre are decoded at once, so a higher sample rate covers more of them
        (2.4 MS/s: the whole band, often 8.8 + 10 MHz or 10 + 11 MHz together).
        Needs HF: RTL-SDR Blog V4, a dongle with an upconverter (or direct sampling), LimeSDR, Airspy with an upconverter.
        Best by day on 8–22 MHz, by night on 3–11 MHz. Each ground station sends a squitter every 32 s on its active channels,
        so the heard stations show up even without aircraft traffic. ↑ = from the ground, ↓ = from the aircraft.
        Blue = aircraft type, flight level, speed and squawk looked up on ADS-B (adsb.fi / adsb.lol, needs internet)
        by the ICAO address or registration; click the flight, registration or ICAO to open the map in a new tab.
        Over the oceans aircraft are often out of ADS-B coverage: then "not seen on ADS-B now".
        Ground station: the one the message is addressed to (↓) or sent by (↑), from the HFDL frame header.
        A click on a message marks it and holds the list still (SCROLL PAUSED); the mouse wheel over the messages resumes it;
        a long message shows 3 lines, a click shows all. AIRCRAFT: the newest data of each aircraft; MAP: aircraft with a position and their tracks.

AIS (Automatic Identification System): position reports, static and voyage data of ships, base stations,
        aids to navigation and SAR aircraft, GMSK 9600 Bd on 161.975 MHz (AIS 1) and 162.025 MHz (AIS 2), decoded here in RADIMCOM

        A click on a message marks it and holds the list still (SCROLL PAUSED); the mouse wheel over the messages resumes it.
        Each message also has its NMEA !AIVDM sentence (in the tooltip of the message type and in the LOG).

HISTORY

        
        
      

      
Weather balloons: radiosondes send their GPS position, altitude, speed and climb once a second on
        400–406 MHz while they rise to 30–35 km, burst and fall back on a parachute. Decoded here from the baseband,
        independent of the listening mode and filter: tune the VFO onto the sonde (a few kHz off is fine, Offset in the status shows how far).
        Type: Vaisala RS41 (GFSK 4800 Bd), the sonde of most weather services around (Wien 11035, Prostějov, Budapest 403.2 MHz,
        Poprad-Gánovce 400.6 MHz, Praha), launched at about 23:15 and 11:15 UTC. Reed–Solomon corrects up to 12 wrong bytes in each half of a frame.
        Experimental: Graw DFM (2500 Bd; serial, type DFM-06 / 09 / 17 / PS-15 and temperature after a few seconds, until then
        DFM-<frequency>) and Meteomodem M10 / M20 (9600 Bd, a short wide flash every second on the waterfall;
        needs a bandwidth of 2.048 MHz or more, a weak one gives a position every few seconds; serial as on the label;
        temperature, humidity and M20 pressure).
        Frame layouts, serials and DFM temperature after rs1729's decoders (github.com/rs1729/RS, used by radiosonde_auto_rx).
        Temperature and humidity (RS41): the sonde sends its calibration in 51 pieces, one a second, so the temperature appears
        after up to a minute of reception and humidity soon after; humidity is approximate (a few %). Pressure (RS41-SGP) is not decoded.
        Range: line of sight, a high sonde is heard 300–500 km away; on the ground only a few km.
        Distance, azimuth and elevation are from the station location (MENU › SETUP › LOCATION).
        While SONDE is on, the audio of the VFO is muted (MUTE in the VFO list): switch SONDE off to listen.
        MULTI: every sonde signal in the visible part of the waterfall (inside 400–406 MHz, up to 6) gets its own decoder,
        so several sondes are decoded at once; their frequencies, type, serial and level are listed above the table.
        Zoom out (or a wider bandwidth) to watch more of the band. HISTORY 1D / 7D adds the flights of the last day / week from the log.

ADS-B and Mode S: the transponders of aircraft answer the radars and broadcast their identity, position,
        altitude and speed on 1090 MHz (pulse position modulation, 1 Mbit/s, 56 or 112 bits with a 24-bit CRC), decoded here in RADIMCOM
        (no external program). ON takes the VFO to 1090.000 MHz at 2.4 MS/s (an Airspy stays at 2.5 MS/s); the audio is muted and
        ACARS, VDL2, DAB, ISM, HFDL and AIS are turned off, because they need the dongle elsewhere. Turning ADS-B off gives the previous sample rate back.
        Messages: DF17 / DF18 ADS-B (flight, category, airborne and surface position, velocity, vertical rate, squawk and emergency,
        selected altitude and QNH), DF11 all-call replies and the radar replies DF0 / 4 / 5 / 16 / 20 / 21 (altitude, squawk) of the
        aircraft already heard. Positions are CPR-decoded from an even and an odd message (or from the last position); distance and azimuth
        from the station location (SETTINGS › GENERAL › LOCATION). One or a few wrong bits of a known aircraft are corrected with the CRC (counted as "fixed").
        Range: line of sight, 100–300 km with a 1090 MHz antenna outdoors; a short whip indoors hears the nearest aircraft only.
        Country from the ICAO address; a click on the ICAO address opens globe.adsb.fi, on the flight adsb.lol, in a new tab.
        An aircraft not heard for 1 min is grey, after 10 min it is removed.
        RANGE (MAP) draws the farthest position decoded in each 2° sector from the station, for aircraft up to
        10000 ft (green), 20000 ft (yellow), 30000 ft (orange) and at any altitude (violet); it is collected the whole time ADS-B
        runs, kept in data\adsb_range.json between restarts, and starts again when the station location moves.
        Only positions confirmed by an earlier position of the same aircraft count. CLR RANGE (twice) starts it again.
        Registration and type of each aircraft are looked up once on adsb.fi (adsb.lol when adsb.fi does not answer; needs internet);
        an aircraft heard here without a position of its own (radar replies only, or no position messages yet) gets the position
        adsb.fi sees, every 30 s: blue in the list and a blue aircraft on the map.

AGC+

                
                

                  

                  CLIP
                

                
                

                  HEADROOM
                  FLOOR
                  SNRchannel
                  
                

                
                
                

                  

                

                

                

                  
headroom
A/D headroom: how far the 99.9 % peak of the samples stays under the top of the A/D converter
                    (one spike does not count). The target depends on the signal: narrow channel 14 dB, FM broadcast 9, DAB 7.5, ADS-B 3
                    (from the 2 s peak-hold); HF +3, FM band (not WFM) +3, VHF +1, above 1 GHz −2; a band much wider than the channel +2.

                  
floor
Noise floor over the A/D's own noise (its quantisation noise, 8 bits RTL, 12 bits Airspy / Lime):
                    under the goal (narrow 10 dB, FM 5, DAB 8, ADS-B 12; HF +2) the weak signals drown in the A/D; far over it more
                    gain buys nothing (gain wasted) and only takes room from strong signals.

                  
%
Clipping: the share of the samples of one block that hit the top of the A/D (distortion and false
                    signals). 0.01 % = 1 sample in 10 000.

                  
s · blocks
Time: between steps up in seconds, settle in blocks of samples (1 block ≈ 55–130 ms by the bandwidth).

                

                
                
AGC+ key (GAIN) = AUTO: the tuner gain keeps the A/D headroom at its target instead of driving the
                  A/D to the top (that gave intermodulation on HF and near FM stations). An overload (clipping above the limit) cuts at once –
                  at least cut, 6 / 9 / 14 / 30 dB when 0.1 / 1 / 5 / 20 % of the samples clip – and leaves a ceiling 3 dB under that
                  gain for 20 s (90 s after three overloads in a minute); then the gain climbs back to where it was (4 dB per 1.2 s).
                  Too hot (headroom under the target − dead band): down by the error. Room: up by at most up every between steps,
                  not while the noise floor is gain wasted over its goal (3 dB more: a step down); inside the dead band a floor under
                  its goal raises the gain 3 dB. After a step down, a step up needs 1.5× the dead band for 8 s. The first 8 s after a start or a
                  retune climb faster. The meter strip shows the A/D level, the clip lamp, the numbers, the first
                  warning and why the gain is where it is. Inspired by the auto gain of OnAir (adswill/OnAir, the ideas, no code).
                  PRESETS from conservative to bold, the one matching the sliders is lit.
                  With WFM a block with a gain step is played in mono (the step would turn the stereo into noise).
                  Smooth ALC gain (on by default, for this VFO): the audio ALC in AM/LSB/USB/CW raises its gain in a smooth ramp
                  instead of steps, so it does not click and pump; it still falls at once. It is the audio gain after the demodulator,
                  not the tuner gain (the tuner has only fixed steps).

              

              
              

                
Where AGC+ and ALC work

                
                
Top: the path of the signal. AGC+ reads the raw A/D samples and sets only the tuner gain (green), before any filter,
                  so it also sees strong signals outside the channel. The ALC is the audio gain after the demodulator (AM, SSB, CW; FM and WFM have
                  a constant level). Middle: what AUTO does with each block – an overload first, then the A/D headroom, the noise floor
                  over the A/D's own noise limits it; the triangles show the last block. Bottom: an overload and the way back. Click a block or a setting to find its slider or card.

NB – Noise Blanker (NB key)

                

                  
                  
                

                
Now

                

                
The NB key (AUDIO, beside NTH; all modes except WFM and DAB) blanks pulses of ignition, electric
                  fences, switching supplies or radar in the wideband samples of the dongle, before the channel filter, where a
                  pulse is still a few µs long (after the filter it is smeared over milliseconds). A pulse stands out of the
                  envelope of the band (13 µs average); only short ones (up to 100 µs) are blanked, so a strong station is never
                  touched. Level 1–10: 1 only strong pulses, 10 also weak ones (above 8 an occasional noise peak is
                  blanked too). Width: blanked on each side of the pulse (longer for ringing pulses). Now: pulses
                  a second and the share of the samples blanked. The waterfall shows the band unblanked.

              

              

                
HUM – mains hum filter (HUM key)

                

                  
                  
                

                
Now

                

                
The HUM key (AUDIO, under NB; AM/LSB/USB) takes out the hum some stations carry from the mains of
                  their transmitter or studio: narrow notches at the mains frequency and its multiples (50, 100, 150, 200 … Hz;
                  a rectifier hums at 100 Hz and its harmonics), only on those where the hum is (a steady tone over 1 s;
                  caught in ~1–1.5 s), so speech and music without hum stay untouched and nothing echoes. The mains of a transmitter is not exactly 50 Hz: the filter follows it (±1 Hz;
                  Now shows it and how much is taken out). Mains: 50 Hz (Europe, Asia, Africa), 60 Hz (the
                  Americas). Width of each notch: wider takes more of a hum that fades with the station. First in the audio chain, before NF, DNR and the ALC.

              

              

                
DNR – Digital Noise Reduction (DNR key)

                

                  
                

                
Now

                

                
The DNR key (AUDIO, under NTH; AM/LSB/USB/CW) takes the noise out of the audio all the time, also
                  under speech; NTH only turns down the pauses between words (both can be on). In the short-time spectrum
                  (27 ms frames) the noise of each band is learned from its minimum over the last 1.5 s, and each band is lowered
                  by how much noise it holds (Wiener gain, decision-directed SNR: the rest of the noise stays smooth, no warbling
                  tones). After NF, before the ALC. Level 1–10: the noise lowered by at most 6–30 dB (3: 11 dB, the default);
                  higher levels also take a little of weak speech. Mouse wheel over the DNR key changes the level.
                  Now shows how much the noise is lowered.

              

              

                
ALC – audio level (ALC key)

                

                  
                  

                

                  
                  
                

                

                  
                  
                

                

                
The ALC lowers the audio gain at once on a louder signal; after it the gain holds and then
                  comes back up with the rise time constant (−10 dB back in ~hold + 1.15× rise). FAST (default) uses the FAST times,
                  SLOW the SLOW ones; while NTH › EXP › FAST is on, FAST is used by itself: weak speech after a loud part (a strong station, a crash) is loud again sooner, and the noise
                  lifted between words is turned down by the expander. For this VFO.

              

              

                
NTH – Noise Threshold (NTH key)

                
                
                
                
Now

                

                  
                  
                  
                

                

                
                
The NTH key (AUDIO, beside ALC; AM/LSB/USB/CW) switches on the ticked parts.
                  EXP turns the noise between words down by the depth; speech stays at full level. A voice detector
                  looks at the spectrum every 8 ms (speech has harmonics standing out of the noise, the noise is flat), opens at
                  once on speech, holds a while and closes in 80 ms. In CW it watches only the CW passband.

                
AUTO (default): a noise blip lowers the sensitivity, missed weak speech raises it, a chop
                  (reopening soon after closing) makes the hold longer (180–350 ms), the depth follows the measured SNR (8–18 dB).
                  Settles in about 2–3 s, after a retune it starts again fast; Now shows the values in use. AUTO off: depth 3–30 dB (12),
                  sensitivity 1–10 (5; chopped speech → raise, noise opening it → lower), hold 20–1000 ms (100; chopped words → longer).
                  The SNR in Now (speech vs. noise between words) is measured in both modes.

                
FAST (on by default) for weak signals: the voice detector smooths less (the syllable is
                  caught one step sooner), the audio goes out ~8 ms later so the opening comes before the syllable, it stays open
                  down to 1/3 of the threshold (normally 1/2), holds at least 250 ms and closes in 150 ms – fewer drops in the
                  middle of a word.

                
LIM: soft limiter instead of hard clipping; with ALC +4 dB, the peaks rounded, not cut.

              

              

                
AFI – inversion frequency (Hz)

                
                

                
Audio frequency inversion (AFI key): the audio spectrum is mirrored around this frequency
                  (f → 2·F − f, band 0 … 2·F). The same setting scrambles and descrambles. Default 1750 Hz (band up to 3500 Hz). 500–4000 Hz.

              

              

                

                  
                  
                

                

                
Audio filters of the HPF and LPF keys, for this VFO. Faster: turn the mouse wheel over the key.
                  HPF 20–5000 Hz (36 dB per octave), LPF 100–15000 Hz (steep like the filter width: −6 dB at the cutoff, −80 dB 100 Hz above). Together they make a band-pass, so HPF always stays below LPF.
                  What you hear and REC are filtered, decoders are not. Not for DAB.

              

              

                
Auto Squelch ASQ – above the noise (dB)

                
                

                
ASQ key: the squelch level follows the noise in the channel plus this margin. The noise is measured
                  in the spectrum of the dongle band (its quietest points, converted to the channel bandwidth), so a long
                  transmission in the channel does not raise the level and close it. In a crowded band the quietest channel power
                  of the last 15 s is used if it is lower. 2–30 dB, default 6 dB.
                  With AGC+ on, a squelch set by the SQL bar of the VFO window works as auto squelch too (a fixed level would not fit the changing gain);
                  the SQL bar then only turns it on and off.

              

              

                

                  
                  
                

                
Tone squelch in FM, keys TSQ-C (CTCSS, sub-audio tone 67.0–254.1 Hz) and TSQ-D (DCS, digital code
                  at 134.4 bit/s). They add to the squelch of the SQL bar or ASQ: the audio passes only while that squelch is open
                  and the set tone or code is received. Without the SQL bar / ASQ the tone alone opens it. One of them at a time.
                  The tone is filtered out of what you hear (300 Hz high-pass). It opens about 0.3 s after the tone starts.
                  Faster: turn the mouse wheel over the key. The VFO window shows the set tone and code under the frequency:
                  grey = off, orange = on, lit orange box = received now. For this VFO.
